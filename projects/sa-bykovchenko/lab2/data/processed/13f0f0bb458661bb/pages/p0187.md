## 9.11 RADAR

Performance of the rendezvous and landing radars was satisfactory, and antenna temperatures were always within normal limits. Range and velocity were acquired by the landing radar at slant ranges of approximately 44 000 and 28 000 feet, respectively. The tracker was lost briefly at altitudes of 240 and 75 feet; these losses were expected and are attributed to zero-Doppler effects associated with manual maneuvering.

## 9.12 CREW STATION

### 9.12.1 Displays and Controls

The displays and controls satisfactorily supported the mission, except that the mission timer stopped during the descent. After being deenergized for 11 hours, the timer was started again and operated properly throughout the remainder of the mission. The most probable cause of this failure was a cracked solder joint. This anomaly is discussed in greater detail in section 16.2.1.

### 9.12.2 Crew Provisions

The Commander and Lunar Module Pilot were provided with communications carrier adapter eartubes, having molded earpieces, for use in the lunar module cabin. The purpose of these earphone adapters is to increase the audio level to the ear. The Lunar Module Pilot used adapters throughout the lunar module des cent and landing phase, but after landing, he found the molded earpieces uncomfortable and removed them. The Commander did not use adapters since his preflight experience indicated audio volume levels were adequate; the use of the adapters is based on crew preference. The Apollo 10 Lunar Module Pilot had used the adapters during his entire lunar module operational period and reported no discomfort. The Apollo 12 crew will also be provided adapters for optional use.

The crew commented that the inflight coverall garments would be more utilitarian if they were patterned after the standard one-piece sumer flying suit. More pockets with a better method of closure, preferably zippers, were recommended and will be provided for evaluation by future crews.
